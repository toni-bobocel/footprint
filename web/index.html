    How it works

    You tell me what and where, in your own words: "dentists in Porto", "new restaurants near Leeds without a website". Or you click a spot on the globe and pick how far around it to look.

    I ask Overture Maps and OpenStreetMap for everything matching around that point and, in France, the national company register too, which lists businesses that never put themselves on a map. Everything is merged so each business shows up once.

    Then I open each business's home page once, the way a visitor would, and note what's there and what isn't: a site at all, https, speed, a phone layout, social profiles, review links, analytics. The businesses with the most missing come first. They're the ones with the most to gain.

    Nothing is guessed by a model. Every flag comes from a rule you can read in the code.

    Sources

    Overture Maps, the open places dataset published each month by the Overture Maps Foundation: tens of millions of businesses worldwide, often with their website, phone and social links. The API reads only the slice of it around your search.

    OpenStreetMap, through the public Overpass servers. Worldwide, volunteer-mapped, best in cities. When those servers are busy I say so instead of pretending there's nothing there.

    Recherche d'entreprises, the French government's open company register. Companies that opted out of commercial prospecting are never shown.

    Nominatim turns place names into coordinates, at most once a second as its rules ask.

    Google ratings appear only when whoever runs the server adds their own Places API key. I don't scrape Google.
